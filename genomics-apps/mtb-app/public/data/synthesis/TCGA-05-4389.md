## Treatment options

### Current decision: after surgery for stage IA disease, no evidence of disease

**1. Observation with surveillance imaging (recommended).** No FDA label supports adjuvant or other systemic therapy for a patient with resected stage IA non-small cell lung cancer and his findings (see Not indicated, below). Surveillance after curative treatment to detect recurrence is the recommended practice (Guldbrandsen et al., 2026).
- *Considerations:* no surveillance scan is recorded since 2023-02-15, so a current CT is needed first. In one retrospective study of resected disease, KEAP1 mutations were linked to worse overall survival in patients who, like him, had no adjuvant chemotherapy (Emerging evidence), but whether his F246L change disables KEAP1 is unknown, and the study doesn't support any added treatment. He is ECOG 1 with normal organ function.
- *Why first:* it is the only approach the evidence supports in his setting. No adjuvant label covers stage IA disease. He is about 3.75 years from diagnosis, and his surgery date isn't recorded. The adjuvant trials found require enrollment within set windows after surgery or therapy (4–12 weeks, 21–77 days, or 32 weeks).

## Emerging evidence

Searches covered 2026-02-26 to 2026-11-17 (PubMed; FDA approval announcements by web search). None of the items below changes the current recommendation.

- **KEAP1 and relapse after surgery** (Molina-Vila et al., *Eur J Cancer* 2026;245:116583). Retrospective study of 350 resected NSCLC patients, validated in 161 more. In the 161 patients given adjuvant chemotherapy, KEAP1 mutations were linked to shorter time to relapse and overall survival. In the 186 who had no chemotherapy (his situation), only overall survival was worse. If his KEAP1 F246L disables the gene, this supports careful surveillance. It isn't evidence for added treatment.
- **Adjuvant durvalumab, BR.31** (Goss et al., *J Clin Oncol* 2026;44(7):553–564). Randomized trial in resected stage IB (≥4 cm) to IIIA: no disease-free survival difference versus placebo in the primary PD-L1 ≥25% group. Adds to the case against adjuvant immunotherapy outside the labeled stages.
- **Treatments for BRAF non-V600 changes:**
  - **Ulixertinib** (Subbiah et al., *Clin Cancer Res* 2026;32(21):4782–4790). Single-arm NCI-MATCH Z1L: 0% response rate in tumors with BRAF non-V600 mutations or fusions (34 patients, 3 lung).
  - **Dabrafenib plus trametinib, off label** (Torresan et al., *Front Oncol* 2026;16:1717614). Single-center series of 9 NSCLC patients with class II–III BRAF mutations, 4 with G469 (classed IIb). Stable disease at best.

  Both argue for trials rather than off-label BRAF/MEK or ERK drugs at recurrence.
- **CTNNB1 in NSCLC** (Improta et al., *Anticancer Res* 2026;46(7):3699–3707). 14 patients: older age and lower PD-L1 than controls. Small and descriptive, with no treatment implication.
- **Surveillance imaging** (Guldbrandsen et al., *Clin Lung Cancer* 2026;27(3):180–189; secondary analysis of the randomized SUPE_R trial in stage IA–IIIC). PET/CT detected recurrence with higher sensitivity (88% vs 62%) but lower specificity (89% vs 96%) than CT. Relevant to choosing his surveillance imaging.
- **FDA approvals in the window** for NSCLC were for HER2-mutant (zongertinib, sevabertinib) and ROS1-positive (zidesamtinib; taletrectinib, a label update to a drug first approved in June 2025) disease. None applies to his findings.
