## Cancer drivers

**Bottom line: the tumor is driven by an activated KRAS (Q61R) and loss of the APC brake, the classic colorectal pattern; KRAS Q61R has no approved targeted drug in colorectal cancer and it rules out anti-EGFR antibodies.**

| Pathway | Gene | Role | Change | Treatment status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| RTK/RAS | KRAS | Accelerator | Q61R | Trial only (most RAS-directed trials require ECOG 0–1; two do not state a limit) |
| WNT | APC | Brake | R213\* and R1450\* (two truncating variants) | Watch |

KRAS is a switch in the RAS growth-signaling pathway, and Q61R locks it in the "on" position, so the cell keeps receiving a signal to divide without any outside growth signal. That is also why blocking the EGF receptor upstream (cetuximab, panitumumab) does not help. APC is the main brake on the WNT pathway in the colon. Two separate truncating variants suggest both copies of APC are disabled, as is typical of colorectal cancer, although the data cannot show whether they sit on different copies. Together, APC loss and KRAS activation are the most common driver pair in colorectal cancer. The SMAD4, CDK12 and GRIN2A changes are of uncertain effect and are not counted as drivers. The chromosome 10 loss removes only one copy of PTEN and other brakes, which is not enough to count without a second hit. The variant allele fractions (0.08–0.38) cannot show whether each driver is in all tumor cells, because tumor purity is not reported. The app's Pathways view shows these pathways.

## Treatment options

Current decision: third-line therapy for KRAS-mutant, BRAF wild-type, mismatch-repair-proficient colorectal cancer. He has progressed after fluoropyrimidine, oxaliplatin, irinotecan and bevacizumab. As a RAS-mutant tumor, he does not need prior anti-EGFR therapy to meet these labels. The three options are given in sequence, not as alternatives: after one fails, the next may be used.

**1. Trifluridine/tipiracil with bevacizumab** (Lonsurf, label 2025-11-05)
- *Support:* The label covers metastatic colorectal cancer previously treated with fluoropyrimidine-, oxaliplatin- and irinotecan-based chemotherapy, an anti-VEGF biological therapy, and anti-EGFR therapy only if RAS wild-type. He meets this, provided the recurrence is metastatic. Per a 2026 report, the phase 3 SUNLIGHT trial showed better overall survival with the combination than with trifluridine/tipiracil alone. A 2026 network meta-analysis ranked the combination first among later-line options (overall survival HR 0.41 vs placebo).
- *Considerations:* Bevacizumab in second line was stopped on 2026-02-07 for absent response, while FOLFIRI continued. The label nonetheless covers bevacizumab with trifluridine/tipiracil after prior anti-VEGF therapy. Trifluridine/tipiracil alone is covered by the same label if bevacizumab is not wanted. CIViC level B (EID1139, a 12-study meta-analysis) found lower response rates and shorter progression-free and overall survival with bevacizumab-based chemotherapy in KRAS-mutant tumors than in KRAS wild-type tumors; that means less benefit, not no benefit. Neutropenia is the main toxicity. Current blood counts are adequate (neutrophils 5.9, hemoglobin 10.4, platelets 298). With ECOG 2 at 82, the shorter 14-day schedule under Emerging evidence may matter.
- *Why first:* labeled for his exact setting, with randomized survival evidence for the combination, and in a 2026 real-world study of patients ≥65 it was better tolerated than regorafenib.

**2. Fruquintinib** (Fruzaqla, label 2026-07-31)
- *Support:* The label covers metastatic colorectal cancer previously treated with fluoropyrimidine-, oxaliplatin- and irinotecan-based chemotherapy, an anti-VEGF therapy and, if RAS wild-type and medically appropriate, an anti-EGFR therapy. He meets this. In the 2026 network meta-analysis, overall survival HR was 0.66 vs placebo overall and 0.71 (95% CrI 0.51–1.01) in KRAS-mutant tumors.
- *Considerations:* Oral, single agent. It needs blood pressure and urine protein monitoring (neither recorded) and a bleeding history.
- *Why second:* labeled with placebo-controlled survival evidence. It ranks below option 1 because single agents ranked below the combination in the 2026 network meta-analysis. It is close to regorafenib; the meta-analysis found no established superiority among the single agents.

**3. Regorafenib** (Stivarga, label 2026-02-09)
- *Support:* The label covers metastatic colorectal cancer previously treated with fluoropyrimidine-, oxaliplatin- and irinotecan-based chemotherapy, an anti-VEGF therapy and, if RAS wild-type, an anti-EGFR therapy. He meets this.
- *Considerations:* His genomic findings point both ways. CIViC level B (submitted) items from one 76-patient retrospective cohort link APC mutation with response to regorafenib (EID13262) and SMAD4 mutation with shorter progression-free survival on it (EID13265). His SMAD4 change is a missense variant of uncertain effect. In the 2026 network meta-analysis, regorafenib's overall survival HR in KRAS-mutant tumors was 0.81 (95% CrI 0.52–1.19) vs placebo. In a 2026 real-world study of patients ≥65, 30.4% stopped regorafenib for adverse events.

## Emerging evidence

Searches covered 2025-10-06 to 2026-11-17 (PubMed, FDA announcements via the web, and meeting reports).

- **Zanzalintinib with atezolizumab vs regorafenib (STELLAR-303).** Phase 3 trial in previously treated metastatic colorectal cancer that is not MSI-H/dMMR (Lancet, 2025). Overall survival improved: median 10.9 vs 9.4 months, HR 0.80 (95% CI 0.69–0.93). Grade ≥3 treatment-related adverse events occurred in 60% vs 37%. The FDA accepted the application with a decision date of 2026-12-03 (OncLive, 2026-02-02). After updated safety and efficacy data were classed as a major amendment, the FDA extended the decision date to 2027-03-03 (OncLive and Targeted Oncology, 2026-09-11; CancerNetwork, 2026-09-14). Zanzalintinib is not approved today. If approved, it would be a later-line option for his mismatch-repair-proficient disease, but its higher rate of severe toxicity matters at ECOG 2.
- **14-day vs 28-day trifluridine/tipiracil with bevacizumab (PRABITAS).** Phase 3 non-inferiority trial in Japan (Lancet, 2026; 970 patients). Median overall survival was 11.3 months on the 14-day schedule vs 10.3 months on the standard 28-day schedule (HR 0.93), with less grade ≥3 neutropenia (13% vs 39%). This bears on giving option 1 to an older patient with ECOG 2. The label's schedule is the 28-day one.
- **Trifluridine/tipiracil with bevacizumab vs regorafenib in patients aged ≥65.** Retrospective study, 81 patients (Cancers, 2026). Median overall survival was 12.5 vs 6.5 months, and stopping for adverse events occurred in 2.9% vs 30.4%. This is real-world evidence that supports the ranking above.
- **KRAS status and later-line drugs.** In a network meta-analysis of 10 randomized trials (Frontiers in Oncology, 2026), the overall survival estimates against placebo in KRAS-mutant tumors favored fruquintinib (HR 0.71) and TAS-102 (HR 0.74) but not regorafenib (HR 0.81, credible interval crossing 1). A pooled analysis of five placebo-controlled third-line trials (Clinical Colorectal Cancer, 2026) found that KRAS mutation was associated with worse overall survival in patients treated with trifluridine/tipiracil or regorafenib (HR 1.18) but not in those given placebo (HR 1.03); it did not report treatment benefit within KRAS-mutant tumors. A retrospective cohort (Clinical and Translational Oncology, 2026) found that KRAS codon 61 mutations had the poorest overall survival of the KRAS subtypes. All three are hypothesis-generating; none changes which drug is labeled.
- **Daraxonrasib.** This oral RAS(ON) inhibitor received its first FDA approval on 2026-08-26, for previously treated metastatic pancreatic cancer (Drugs, 2026). It is being developed in colorectal cancer. No colorectal results were found in the search window. For him it remains trial-only, and the trials need ECOG 0–1.
