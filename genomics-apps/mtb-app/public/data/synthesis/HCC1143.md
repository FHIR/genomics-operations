First distant recurrence of triple-negative breast cancer: new lung and mediastinal nodal metastases (CT 2026-08-24), biopsy-confirmed on 2026-09-09, more than 21 months after adjuvant chemotherapy ended (2024-11-04). The metastasis is PD-L1 positive (22C3, combined positive score 12). No treatment for metastatic disease is recorded, so this is a first-line decision.

- What first-line therapy is supported for PD-L1-positive (CPS 12) metastatic triple-negative breast cancer?

How this fits together: the two reported mutations were both found in the genomic data at the same positions. The genomic data add a PTEN exon deletion that the report doesn't mention; the report states "no pathogenic alterations in PTEN", probably because it covered small mutations only, so the deletion should be confirmed (see Information gaps). Every variant in the data has the same allele fraction (25%), which looks like a placeholder, so how many tumor cells carry each change can't be judged.

- **Germline vs somatic:** TP53 p.Arg248Gln is classified pathogenic for Li-Fraumeni syndrome when inherited, but here it was found in the tumor and not in normal tissue, and the germline panel (which included TP53) was negative. A somatic TP53 mutation doesn't contradict that negative germline result.
- **Report implications:** the report's tamoxifen (level B) and sunitinib (level B) implications match CIViC. Its pemigatinib implication (level D, preclinical) couldn't be found in CIViC: CIViC's pemigatinib evidence covers FGFR2 fusions (level A, cholangiocarcinoma; EID8173) and one resistance mutation, not p.Arg203Cys.
- **Spanned, not broken:** large rearrangements span several genes a reader may check in the Genomics Explorer, but don't break them, so they aren't counted. NTRK1, PIK3CA, BRAF and PALB2 lie inside large inversions; RET, MET and MLH1 lie inside large duplications, with no copy number recorded to call an amplification. An inversion breakpoint lies about 0.8 kb beyond the 3′ end of PMS2's main transcript; it falls inside the gene's longest annotated span, so the Explorer shows it under PMS2, but it doesn't break the PMS2 coding sequence and isn't counted. Small deletions inside FHIT, WWOX and ASXL1 remove only intron sequence. An 11-base deletion in TP53 lies in the gene's 3′ untranslated region, after the protein-coding sequence, so it doesn't change the protein and isn't counted. The Explorer may also show a few records whose recorded positions lie outside the gene searched (for example a variant listed under NF1); they aren't in those genes and aren't counted.

- 20 other small variants in cancer genes were reviewed and aren't listed: mostly intronic changes and missense changes of unknown effect, plus a truncating change in NUP98, a gene that drives cancer only when switched on (by fusion), so a truncation isn't counted. None has known significance.

## Cancer drivers

**Bottom line: the tumor is driven mainly by loss of the TP53 brake, with loss of the PTEN brake and an activating FGFR2 change; none of these has an approved targeted drug in triple-negative breast cancer, so first-line treatment rests on PD-L1 status, not on these drivers.**

| Pathway | Gene | Role | Change | Treatment status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| TP53 | TP53 | Brake | p.Arg248Gln hotspot mutation | Watch |
| PI3K growth signaling | PTEN | Brake | Exon 6 deletion | Trial only (after first-line) |
| RTK-RAS growth signaling | FGFR2 | Accelerator | p.Arg203Cys hotspot mutation | Trial only (after first-line) |
| Other | MAGI2 | Brake | Two exon deletions | Watch |
| Other | HDAC4 | Brake | Splice-site change | Watch |

TP53 is the cell's main damage checkpoint; this hotspot mutation disables it. Losing PTEN releases the brake on the PI3K growth pathway, and the FGFR2 change switches on a growth-factor receptor that feeds the RAS growth pathway, so two separate growth signals are turned up in a tumor that has already lost its checkpoint. MAGI2 and HDAC4 are minor drivers with little known clinical meaning. Whether the second copy of PTEN (and of TP53) is also lost can't be told, because copy number wasn't recorded; PTEN immunohistochemistry would show whether PTEN protein is gone. Whether each change is in all tumor cells (clonal) can't be known from these data, and the profile is from the 2024 primary tumor, not the lung metastasis. The app's Pathways view shows these pathways.

## Information gaps

- **Copy number:** the structural data record no copy-number values, so amplifications and gene losses can't be called. Whether PTEN and TP53 have lost their second copy, and whether BRCA2 and RB1 have lost one copy, is unknown. PTEN immunohistochemistry would confirm PTEN loss and would also meet one trial's entry route (below).
- **PTEN deletion not on the report:** the exon 6 deletion comes from the genomic data only; confirmation by a clinical laboratory would be needed before it is used for trial eligibility.
- **Fusions:** the tests don't detect translocations, so fusions (NTRK, RET, FGFR2, NRG1) were not tested; no inversion or deletion breaks these genes. RNA-based fusion testing would be needed to rule them out.
- **Where the small-variant test couldn't make calls:** the test studied every region searched but didn't report its uncallable regions, so a missing small variant is "not found" rather than proven absent.
- **Genes that couldn't be searched:** NCOA4, PDE4DIP and part of CRLF2 couldn't be searched because their coordinates don't convert between genome builds. None is relevant to the current decision (NCOA4 matters only as a RET fusion partner, and RET isn't broken).
- **Which sample the genomic data come from:** the genomic data list two tests dated February 2020 on a specimen labeled "HCC1143-Sp1", while the report is dated 2024-06-14 on the partial mastectomy. The structural data also include about ten events on the Y chromosome, which a female patient wouldn't have. Confirm that the genomic data belong to this tumor before acting on findings seen only there (the PTEN, MAGI2 and HDAC4 changes).
- **Metastasis not profiled:** profiling was done on the 2024 primary tumor. The 2026-09-09 lung biopsy is available if re-profiling is wanted; changes acquired since 2024 are unknown.
- **Tumor mutational burden:** not measured. It isn't needed for the first-line options, but a high value (≥13 mutations/Mb by an FDA-authorized test) would support pembrolizumab in later lines.
- **UGT1A1 genotype:** the sacituzumab govitecan label warns that patients homozygous for UGT1A1\*28 have more neutropenia and anemia. This is an inherited trait; the tumor test reports somatic changes only and found nothing in UGT1A1, so her genotype is unknown. This matters more because her hemoglobin is already low (11.6 g/dL).
- **Other first-line details:** whether she has peripheral neuropathy after paclitaxel (affects the choice of a taxane), whether her hypothyroidism is autoimmune (relevant to immunotherapy and to the TROPION-Breast05 trial, which excludes prior autoimmune disorders) or she has other autoimmune history, and hepatitis B and C status (one PTEN trial excludes any history of hepatitis B or C) aren't recorded.

## Treatment options

### First-line options

1. **Sacituzumab govitecan plus pembrolizumab** (Trodelvy label 2026-06-24 and Keytruda label 2026-10-16: first-line unresectable locally advanced or metastatic TNBC with PD-L1 CPS ≥10 by an FDA-authorized test).
   - Evidence: phase 3 ASCENT-04 (443 previously untreated PD-L1-positive patients). Progression-free survival 11.2 vs 7.8 months with pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy (hazard ratio 0.65); overall survival data immature; treatment stopped for side effects in 12% vs 31%.
   - *Considerations:* pembrolizumab can be given IV or as subcutaneous Keytruda Qlex (pembrolizumab and berahyaluronidase alfa), labeled for the same indication. She meets the label: CPS 12 by 22C3 pharmDx on the metastasis, no prior treatment for metastatic disease, and recurrence well beyond the trial's minimum 6 months after curative treatment. ECOG 1 and normal liver and kidney function. Neutropenia and diarrhea are the main risks; her hemoglobin is already low and her UGT1A1 genotype is unknown (see Information gaps). Hypothyroidism on levothyroxine needs thyroid monitoring with pembrolizumab.
   - *Why first:* labeled for exactly her situation, and it beat the second option head-to-head on progression-free survival with fewer discontinuations. It ranks above the second option on that randomized comparison, although its survival benefit isn't proven yet.
2. **Pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy (paclitaxel, nab-paclitaxel, or gemcitabine plus carboplatin)** (Keytruda label 2026-10-16: locally recurrent unresectable or metastatic TNBC with PD-L1 CPS ≥10 by an FDA-authorized test).
   - Evidence: phase 3 KEYNOTE-355 (CPS ≥10 group): overall survival 23 vs 16.1 months with chemotherapy alone (hazard ratio 0.73); progression-free survival 9.7 vs 5.6 months.
   - *Considerations:* she meets the label. She had weekly paclitaxel ending 2024-11-04; her neuropathy status should be checked before choosing a taxane, and gemcitabine plus carboplatin is the non-taxane partner. Same thyroid monitoring as above.
   - *Why second:* proven survival benefit over chemotherapy alone, but inferior progression-free survival to option 1 in ASCENT-04. A reasonable choice if sacituzumab govitecan's side effects are a concern.

Co-occurring alterations: CIViC evidence for combinations of her alterations was checked and none applies at level A or B. The only partial match (PTEN, BRCA2 and TP53 deletions together, olaparib, level C, uterine leiomyosarcoma; she has a TP53 mutation and a small deletion outside its protein-coding sequence, not loss of the gene, and only possible single-copy BRCA2 loss) isn't a basis for treatment.

### After first-line therapy

1. **Sacituzumab govitecan alone**, if not used first-line (Trodelvy label 2026-06-24: unresectable locally advanced or metastatic TNBC after two or more prior systemic therapies, at least one for metastatic disease). Her adjuvant chemotherapy plus a first-line regimen would meet the label's wording. However, in the supporting trial a (neo)adjuvant regimen counted as a prior line only if the disease recurred within 12 months of it; she recurred about 21½ months after adjuvant chemotherapy, so her situation after one metastatic line is less directly supported by the trial data.
2. **Trials matched to her genomic findings** (table below): an FGFR inhibitor combination for activating FGFR mutations, and two PTEN-directed trials.

### Not indicated by the patient's findings

- **Not recommended: olaparib (Lynparza) or talazoparib (Talzenna).** Labeled only for inherited (germline) BRCA mutations; her germline panel was negative and the tumor has no BRCA1 or BRCA2 mutation.
- **Trastuzumab deruxtecan (Enhertu):** labeled for HER2-positive or HER2-low (IHC 1+ or 2+) breast cancer, or HER2-ultralow only when hormone-receptor-positive. Her tumor is HER2 IHC 0 and hormone-receptor-negative.
- **Datopotamab deruxtecan (Datroway) or sacituzumab govitecan alone as first-line:** labeled for TNBC patients who are not candidates for PD-1/PD-L1 therapy; with CPS 12 she is a candidate.
- **Atezolizumab (Tecentriq):** the current label (2026-10-13) has no breast cancer indication.
- **Capivasertib (Truqap) for the PTEN deletion:** labeled only for hormone-receptor-positive breast cancer; CIViC's level A evidence (capivasertib plus fulvestrant, EID12020) is also from hormone-receptor-positive disease. In TNBC, capivasertib plus paclitaxel didn't improve survival even in PTEN-altered tumors (see Emerging evidence).
- **Pemigatinib (Pemazyre) for FGFR2 p.Arg203Cys:** labeled for FGFR2 fusions in cholangiocarcinoma and FGFR1 rearrangements in myeloid/lymphoid neoplasms, not FGFR2 point mutations or breast cancer.
- **Not recommended: sunitinib.** CIViC level B evidence in FGFR-altered breast cancer shows it doesn't work (response rate 7%; EID11883).
- **Tamoxifen:** her tumor is ER-negative, and CIViC level B evidence links TP53 DNA-binding mutations to tamoxifen resistance (EID2783).
- **Not recommended: bevacizumab or other VEGF/VEGFR inhibitors for the TP53 mutation.** CIViC level B pan-cancer evidence (EID11709) comes from a phase 1 analysis of disease control, not breast cancer, and the bevacizumab label (Avastin, 2025-01-06) has no breast cancer indication.
- **Eprenetapopt plus pembrolizumab for the TP53 mutation:** CIViC level B pan-cancer evidence (EID12033, phase 1b, clinical benefit in 13.8%); eprenetapopt isn't FDA-approved and is available only in trials.
- **Sapanisertib for the PTEN deletion:** CIViC level B evidence in solid tumors with mTOR/PI3K/PTEN/AKT mutations (EID12075, 30 patients, 4 partial responses); sapanisertib isn't FDA-approved and is available only in trials, and it isn't established that an exon deletion counts as the "PTEN mutation" that study enrolled. Everolimus and buparlisib-based regimens were ineffective in PTEN-deficient tumors (CIViC level B, EID5840, EID5957).
- **Tissue-agnostic options:** her tumor is microsatellite stable, has no BRAF V600E, no NTRK or RET rearrangement found, and is HER2 IHC 0. Pembrolizumab's tumor-mutational-burden indication doesn't apply because TMB wasn't measured (and her PD-L1 indication already covers pembrolizumab).

## Emerging evidence

Searches covered 2025-01-06 to 2026-11-16 (PubMed, FDA oncology approval notices, ASCO and ESMO reports).

- **ASCENT-04 second progression (ASCO 2026, abstract LBA1000, 2026-06-02; exploratory post hoc analysis of the phase 3 trial):** time to progression on the next line of therapy wasn't reached with sacituzumab govitecan plus pembrolizumab vs 21 months with chemotherapy plus pembrolizumab (2-year rate 63.7% vs 45.6%), even though 81% of the chemotherapy group who went on to another treatment received sacituzumab govitecan next. This is consistent with using sacituzumab govitecan first-line rather than saving it, but as an exploratory analysis it doesn't settle the sequencing question; overall survival is still not reported.
- **CAPItello-290 (Annals of Oncology 2026; phase 3):** capivasertib plus paclitaxel first-line in metastatic TNBC didn't improve overall survival overall (17.7 vs 18.0 months) or in PIK3CA/AKT1/PTEN-altered tumors (20.4 months in both arms). This argues against an AKT inhibitor for her PTEN deletion.
- **FGFR inhibitors by alteration type (European Journal of Cancer 2026; retrospective, 158 patients):** FGFR point mutations had a 30.9% response rate (mostly urothelial cancer), compared with 45.9% for fusions and 11.1% for amplifications (found mainly in breast cancer). Results for FGFR2 p.Arg203Cys weren't reported. Some support for an FGFR inhibitor trial later, but no evidence specific to her mutation, and FGFR-altered breast cancers in this series were mostly amplified and responded poorly.
