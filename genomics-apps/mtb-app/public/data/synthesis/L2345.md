EGFR is the accelerator that started this cancer, and it stays switched on (L858R persists). Osimertinib held it for about three years. T790M has now appeared too, but osimertinib was designed to block EGFR carrying T790M, so the escape more likely comes from switching on the same growth pathway further downstream, where blocking EGFR no longer helps: two KRAS changes, BRAF V600E and extra MET copies. Having several escape routes at once usually means several resistant cell populations, each needing a different drug. The brakes are also damaged: STK11 appears lost on both copies (a frameshift and a deletion; whether they are on different copies can't be told), which in lung adenocarcinoma is linked to poor response to immunotherapy, and BRCA2 has a stop change in one copy; whether its second copy is lost is unknown. ALK F1245V sits at a recurrent mutation site (seen in a few brain tumors, with drug evidence only in neuroblastoma), but its role in lung cancer is unknown, and the MYC gain is low-level. Worth confirming: the MET copy number by a method trials accept, and whether BRCA2 Q66* is inherited. Without allele fractions, it can't be known which changes are in the dominant population. ## Information gaps

- **MET copy number by FISH, and c-Met protein (IHC):** the recent phase 3 trial of osimertinib + savolitinib required 10 or more copies by FISH or IHC 3+ in at least 90% of cells (Emerging evidence); 5 copies by sequencing doesn't meet its copy-number criterion, and IHC wasn't done. c-Met IHC is also needed for telisotuzumab vedotin's label and is done at screening for one of the trials listed. Both would sharpen the MET-directed choices.
- **MET exon 14 splice region:** the DNA test studied MET exon 14 itself (no variant found) but not the adjoining intron, where many skipping mutations lie; the 2023 report's negative result predates progression.
- **Fusions at progression:** the DNA test can't detect fusions. The 2026 report states only that there is no ALK fusion; ROS1, RET and NTRK fusions (each with labeled drugs) were last reported absent in 2023.
- **Germline status of BRCA2 Q66*:** this exact change is classified as a pathogenic inherited variant in ClinVar, and for BRCA2 most tumor-detected variants of this kind turn out to be inherited; the ESMO Precision Medicine Working Group recommends germline follow-up for BRCA2 whatever the tumor type. The record has no germline test and no family history. An inherited BRCA2 variant would matter for the patient's relatives and his own cancer risks. A somatic finding wouldn't contradict a negative germline test.
- **BRCA2 second copy:** no copy-number loss is recorded anywhere for this patient, and the regions the test studied in BRCA2 couldn't be retrieved, so loss of the second copy is not tested.
- **TMB and MSI:** neither was measured. MSI-high would open tissue-agnostic immunotherapy (no mismatch-repair gene variant was found in the studied regions, which doesn't establish MSI status); TMB-high (≥13 mutations/Mb with an FDA-authorized test) is a pembrolizumab indication. Both are less relevant given STK11 loss.
- **Clonality and specimen:** no variant allele fractions are recorded, and the genomic data don't link findings to the 2026 liver specimen, so whether BRAF, KRAS and MET changes are in the same cells, and how much of the tumor carries each, can't be judged. Whether the exon 19 frameshift sits on the same EGFR copy as L858R is also unknown. A plasma (ctDNA) test could show which changes dominate across sites.
- **Liver function:** AST 58 and ALT 63 U/L with liver metastases; trial limits (e.g. up to 5 times normal with liver metastases) appear met but need current values.

For the next line of therapy after progression on osimertinib with platinum/pemetrexed:

**1A and 1B (ranked together). Too close to call: 1A has the closer fit to his prior treatment, 1B the stronger trial evidence.**

**1A. Datopotamab deruxtecan (Datroway).** Labeled for EGFR-mutated NSCLC after prior EGFR-directed therapy and platinum chemotherapy (accelerated approval, based on response rate); CIViC level A for EGFR-mutant NSCLC (EID 12522, 12523).
*Considerations:* he meets the label exactly (osimertinib and carboplatin/pemetrexed). It doesn't depend on which resistance change dominates, which suits a tumor with several. Interstitial lung disease and stomatitis need monitoring. **Rank:** best fit to his setting and independent of the resistance mix; evidence is response-rate based.

**1B. Amivantamab with carboplatin and pemetrexed (Rybrevant IV, or Rybrevant Faspro subcutaneous).** Labeled for EGFR L858R NSCLC progressing on or after an EGFR inhibitor.
*Considerations:* amivantamab targets both EGFR and MET, relevant to the MET gain. But his disease progressed on pemetrexed maintenance, so the chemotherapy backbone has been partly exhausted; whether rechallenge with carboplatin after more than 3 years helps is a clinical judgment. Infusion reactions and skin toxicity are common. **Rank:** labeled for this exact progression; fit is weakened by prior platinum/pemetrexed.

**2. BRAF + MEK inhibition: dabrafenib + trametinib (Tafinlar + Mekinist) or encorafenib + binimetinib (Braftovi + Mektovi).** Both labeled for metastatic NSCLC with BRAF V600E; CIViC level A for dabrafenib + trametinib (EID 3017). Vemurafenib alone has CIViC level B evidence in BRAF V600E NSCLC (EID 5958, 43% response in 14 patients; EID 3782) but no lung label; the labeled two-drug combinations are preferred over it.
*Considerations:* he meets the labels, but the evidence comes from tumors driven by BRAF alone. Here BRAF V600E is one of several resistance changes on top of persistent EGFR L858R, and BRAF/MEK inhibitors alone don't block EGFR-driven cells. Co-existing KRAS mutations are linked to resistance to BRAF inhibitors (CIViC co-occurring-variant evidence, level B, but only in melanoma, EID 6264). Adding osimertinib to BRAF/MEK inhibitors is off-label (Emerging evidence). **Rank:** labeled and level A, but likely to treat only part of the tumor.

**3. KRAS G12C inhibitors: sotorasib (Lumakras) or adagrasib (Krazati).** Labeled for KRAS G12C NSCLC after at least one prior systemic therapy (accelerated approvals); CIViC level A for sotorasib (EID 9431).
*Considerations:* he meets the labels, but the tumor also carries KRAS G12A, which these drugs don't inhibit, and BRAF V600E and EGFR L858R, so a G12C inhibitor alone would leave other populations untreated. Divarasib has CIViC level B evidence in KRAS G12C NSCLC (EID 11652) but no FDA label; it is available only in trials, and the divarasib trial found requires untreated disease, so it isn't an option for him now. **Rank:** below option 2 because the second KRAS change is unaddressed and the approvals rest on response rates.

**4. Docetaxel, with or without ramucirumab (Cyramza).** Docetaxel is labeled after platinum failure; ramucirumab with docetaxel after platinum, once EGFR disease has progressed on approved EGFR therapy (both met).
*Considerations:* ECOG 1 and adequate counts support it. Ramucirumab would make him ineligible for the Lung-MAP MET trial (NCT05642572), which excludes prior anti-VEGF therapy. **Rank:** standard later-line chemotherapy, kept for after the targeted and antibody-drug options.

**5. MET inhibitor alone: crizotinib or capmatinib.** Neither is labeled for MET amplification (Xalkori: ALK- or ROS1-positive NSCLC; Tabrecta: MET exon 14 skipping); CIViC level B for MET amplification: crizotinib in NSCLC (EID 7639, amplification defined as ≥6 copies, best response 32%; EID 7502, MET/CEP7 ratio >2.2, response 31%), crizotinib across solid tumors (EID 13391, NCI-MATCH, ≥7 copies, response 14%), and capmatinib in NSCLC (EID 8849: response 7–12% in previously treated patients with fewer than 10 copies, 29% with 10 or more).
*Considerations:* his MET copy number is 5: below the copy numbers enrolled in the crizotinib studies, and in the range (fewer than 10 copies) where capmatinib responses were 7–12%. A MET inhibitor alone wouldn't treat the EGFR, KRAS or BRAF populations. MET amplification with MYC amplification has been linked to capmatinib resistance (CIViC co-occurring-variant evidence, level C, case report, molecular profile 4544, EID 11420); he also has a low-level MYC gain (3 copies). **Rank:** last: weakest fit, and it addresses only one of several resistance changes.

Clinical trials are a reasonable alternative to options 1A and 1B, especially NCT07155187, NCT07100080 and NCT05642572 (which accepts MET amplification by sequencing), below.

## Emerging evidence

Searches covered 2025-03-20 to 2026-11-16 (PubMed, FDA announcements, and 2026 meeting reports). None of the following is yet in a label or in CIViC at level A or B.

- **Osimertinib + savolitinib after osimertinib (SAFFRON, phase 3, *N Engl J Med* 2026):** for EGFR-mutated NSCLC with MET overexpression (IHC 3+ in ≥90% of cells) and/or amplification (≥10 copies by FISH) progressing on osimertinib, osimertinib + savolitinib improved progression-free survival (hazard ratio 0.55) and overall survival (0.68) over platinum/pemetrexed. *Bears on him:* the strongest evidence for keeping osimertinib in a combination, but his MET finding (5 copies by sequencing) is below the trial's amplification cutoff and his c-Met IHC is unknown. Savolitinib has no US label.
- **Izalontamab brengitecan vs chemotherapy (PANKU-Lung01, phase 3, *Lancet* 2026):** after third-generation EGFR inhibitors, progression-free survival 11.1 vs 5.7 months (hazard ratio 0.40); overall survival immature. Enrolled patients without prior chemotherapy, in China. *Bears on him:* he has had chemotherapy, so the result doesn't directly apply; the US trial NCT07100080 tests this drug in his setting.
- **Sacituzumab tirumotecan vs platinum/pemetrexed (OptiTROP-Lung04, phase 3, *N Engl J Med* 2026):** after EGFR inhibitor progression, progression-free survival 8.3 vs 4.3 months and overall survival hazard ratio 0.60. *Bears on him:* supports antibody-drug conjugates after EGFR inhibitors; no US label.
- **Osimertinib + datopotamab deruxtecan after first-line osimertinib (ORCHARD, phase 2, *Ann Oncol* 2026):** response 36–43%, median progression-free survival 9.5–11.7 months; lung inflammation in up to 15% at the higher dose. *Bears on him:* a possible way to keep osimertinib with option 1A; off-label.
- **Osimertinib + chemotherapy after osimertinib progression (COMPEL, randomized, *ESMO Open* 2025):** continuing osimertinib with platinum/pemetrexed after non-CNS progression on first-line osimertinib improved progression-free survival (8.4 vs 4.4 months). *Bears on him:* he progressed while already on osimertinib with pemetrexed, so this doesn't support continuing his current regimen.
- **Acquired BRAF alterations after osimertinib:** osimertinib + selumetinib gave a 7% response rate (ORCHARD, phase 2, *Eur J Cancer* 2026), and encorafenib + binimetinib + osimertinib gave 1 response in 5 patients (single-center series, *JTO Clin Res Rep* 2026). *Bears on him:* adding BRAF/MEK inhibitors to osimertinib has limited support so far.
- **RAS-mediated resistance (*Ann Oncol* 2026):** RAS changes were found in 11% of 312 patients progressing on first-line osimertinib, KRAS G12C in only one; combining osimertinib with newer RAS(ON) inhibitors worked in laboratory models. *Bears on him:* supports RAS(ON) trials such as NCT06162221, though no clinical data in this setting yet.
