The tumor began with one accelerator, EGFR L858R, held in check by osimertinib for more than three years. It has now found several ways around it: MET amplification and BRAF V600E and KRAS mutations each switch on growth signaling that doesn't depend on EGFR, so blocking EGFR alone no longer works. These may sit in different groups of cells, but the data can't show which, because no variant allele frequencies were provided. Loss of STK11 (a brake that also shapes the immune response) makes immunotherapy less likely to work. The BRCA2 change is a separate thread: it's a known inherited (germline) pathogenic variant, so even though the report lists it as somatic, germline testing would matter for him and his family. The Genomics Explorer's Pathways view shows these genes on the RTK/RAS pathway diagram.

## Information gaps

- **Germline origin of BRCA2 Q66\*:** ClinVar lists it as a pathogenic germline variant; no germline test is recorded. Germline testing and genetic counseling would settle it, with implications for relatives.
- **MET amplification by the trial's definition:** copy number 5 comes from the genomic data, not the report; MET-targeted trials require confirmation by their own NGS assay (and some use MET overexpression by IHC).
- **Clonality:** no variant allele frequencies, so the size of the BRAF, KRAS and MET subclones is unknown.
- **Genomic data source:** the genomic data's tests are dated 2023-05-23 but contain the 2026 resistance variants, and record no somatic or germline origin. Which specimen they come from should be confirmed.
- **PD-L1** hasn't been repeated on the 2026 liver biopsy.
- **Fusions:** the 2026 report states no ALK fusion; the genomic data's tests don't detect structural rearrangements other than copy number, so other fusions (e.g. RET, NTRK) can't be ruled out from these data.

## Treatment options

**Second line after progression on osimertinib with chemotherapy** (EGFR L858R, multiple resistance alterations, ECOG 1):

1. **Amivantamab + carboplatin + pemetrexed** (rank 1). *For co-occurring EGFR L858R + MET amplification.* Labeled for EGFR L858R NSCLC that has progressed on or after an EGFR TKI (CIViC level A); amivantamab is a bispecific antibody directed at both EGFR and MET. *Why first:* the labeled option for exactly this setting, and the only labeled treatment that addresses two of his drivers at once. *Considerations:* he has had carboplatin and pemetrexed (4 cycles in 2023, then pemetrexed maintenance until progression), so the same chemotherapy backbone would be repeated; mild liver enzyme elevation should be monitored.
2. **Datopotamab deruxtecan** (rank 2, tied). Labeled for EGFR-mutated NSCLC after prior EGFR-directed therapy and platinum chemotherapy, both of which he has had. *Why second:* labeled for his setting and avoids repeating chemotherapy, but doesn't address MET or the other resistance alterations directly.
2. **Capmatinib + osimertinib in the Lung-MAP EGFR/MET sub-study (NCT05642572)** (rank 2, tied, if MET amplification is confirmed by the trial's assay). *For co-occurring EGFR L858R + MET amplification.* A trial for EGFR-mutant, MET-amplified NSCLC progressing on osimertinib; CIViC co-occurrence evidence for EGFR L858R + MET amplification supports an EGFR inhibitor with capmatinib (gefitinib + capmatinib, level C), and phase 3 evidence for another MET + EGFR combination is emerging (see Emerging evidence). *Why tied second:* it targets both drivers, but its evidence is a trial rather than a label, and eligibility depends on confirming MET amplification.
3. **Single-target treatment for a new alteration** (rank 3, tied): **dabrafenib + trametinib** or **encorafenib + binimetinib** (BRAF V600E), or **sotorasib** or **adagrasib** (KRAS G12C, after ≥ 1 prior therapy). Each is labeled for its alteration in NSCLC. *Why third:* each targets only one of several resistance alterations and leaves the EGFR driver untreated; evidence for combining them with osimertinib is limited to case reports and small series.
4. **Olaparib** (rank 4). Off-label; CIViC level B in BRCA1/2-altered lung carcinoma (TAPUR: response rate 12%, all responses in BRCA2-altered tumors). *Why last:* modest activity, and the BRCA2 variant's role in this tumor is uncertain.

**Co-occurring alterations:** besides options 1 and 2, CIViC co-occurrence evidence for his alterations is level A for osimertinib with EGFR L858R + T790M, which he has already progressed on, and level C for osimertinib *resistance* with BRAF V600E + EGFR L858R + T790M (see Not recommended). No FDA label or level A/B evidence supports combining osimertinib with a BRAF or KRAS G12C inhibitor.

**Clinical trials to consider alongside options 1–2** (table below): the antibody-drug conjugate trials after osimertinib.

**Not recommended:**
- **Continuing osimertinib alone:** he has progressed on it, and CIViC co-occurrence evidence links BRAF V600E with EGFR L858R + T790M to osimertinib resistance (level C).
- **PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitor alone:** EGFR-mutant disease with STK11 loss (CIViC level B, resistance) and PD-L1 TPS 5%.

**Not indicated by his findings:**
- **Capmatinib, tepotinib:** labeled only for MET exon 14 skipping, which isn't present (MET amplification isn't a labeled indication).
- **Erlotinib, gefitinib, afatinib:** T790M (CIViC level A) and KRAS G12C/G12A (level B) predict resistance.
- **ALK inhibitors:** labeled for ALK fusions; ALK F1245V is a point mutation, with evidence only in neuroblastoma.

## Emerging evidence

Searches covering 2024-09-25 to 2026-11-06 (PubMed and web, FDA announcements, ASCO/ESMO/AACR) found:

- **SAFFRON** (phase 3; ESMO 2026, LBA6): savolitinib + osimertinib vs. platinum-pemetrexed in EGFR-mutated NSCLC with MET overexpression and/or amplification after osimertinib. Median PFS 7.6 vs. 5.4 months (HR 0.55); overall survival also improved (topline, August 2026). An NDA was submitted to the FDA in September 2026. Directly relevant to his MET amplification; not yet a labeled option.
- **Ivonescimab + chemotherapy** (HARMONi): for EGFR-mutated NSCLC after a third-generation EGFR TKI; PFS HR 0.52. FDA decision expected by 2026-11-14.
- **BRAF V600E after osimertinib:** small retrospective series and case reports of dabrafenib + trametinib with osimertinib (e.g. response rate 61.5% in one multicenter series); no prospective trial.
