All four drivers are brakes lost; no accelerator (oncogene) was found switched on. Losing TP53 removes the cell's main damage checkpoint, and TP53 mutation carries a poorer prognosis in breast cancer. SMAD4 loss removes a growth-restraining signal. RNF43 normally holds back WNT signaling. ARID1A helps package DNA, and trials of ARID1A-targeted drugs are open only for advanced disease. The inherited PALB2 variant is one hit in a DNA-repair gene. Whether the tumor lost the other PALB2 copy (which would make it more PARP-inhibitor-sensitive) can't be told, because copy number wasn't tested. Each driver has a single hit found, and the second copy of each gene can't be assessed. Variant allele fractions aren't available, so whether these changes are in all tumor cells (clonal) or only some is unknown. The app's Pathways view shows where these genes sit.

## Information gaps

- **Menopausal status** isn't recorded. Anastrozole is labeled for postmenopausal women. Ribociclib with an aromatase inhibitor needs an LHRH agonist in pre- or perimenopausal women. At 53, her status should be documented (and confirmed with labs if uncertain).
- **Medication list and ECG.** Ribociclib prolongs the QT interval and interacts with strong CYP3A inhibitors. Her antidepressant, anxiolytic and antihypertensive drugs aren't recorded, and there's no baseline ECG, liver tests or electrolytes. Allergies aren't recorded.
- **Tumor size vs. T category.** Pathology gives 2.5 cm but pT3 (which means over 5 cm). This doesn't change ribociclib eligibility: as T3N0 she is stage IIB, and as T2N0 grade 2 with a high recurrence score she still meets the trial definition of high risk. The record should be corrected.
- **Ki-67** isn't recorded (not needed here, given the high recurrence score).
- **Copy number and fusions weren't tested.** That leaves untested: loss of the second PALB2 copy, FGFR1 amplification (linked in CIViC to resistance to letrozole plus ribociclib when co-amplified with ZNF703, level B), ERBB2 amplification, and NTRK or RET fusions.
- **HER2 IHC score** isn't recorded (only "negative"). If the disease recurs, HER2-low or HER2-ultralow status would decide eligibility for trastuzumab deruxtecan.
- **MSI, mismatch-repair IHC and TMB** aren't in the record. No variant was found in the mismatch-repair genes, but that can't establish MSI status. These matter only if the disease recurs.
- **Germline testing was limited to seven genes.** With ovarian cancer in a maternal aunt and pancreatic cancer in a paternal uncle, broader germline testing may be offered (ASCO-SSO 2024). ## Treatment options

**Current decision: adjuvant systemic therapy with anastrozole**

1. **Add ribociclib to anastrozole (rank 1).** Kisqali (ribociclib) is labeled, with an aromatase inhibitor, for adjuvant treatment of HR-positive, HER2-negative stage II and III early breast cancer at high risk of recurrence (label 2026-07-01). The pivotal trial counted node-negative disease as high risk when the tumor is over 5 cm, or 2–5 cm with grade 2 and a high genomic risk. She is stage IIB, node-negative, grade 2, with a recurrence score of 47, so she meets the label either way. Dosing is 400 mg daily, 3 weeks on and 1 week off, for up to 3 years, with the aromatase inhibitor for at least 5 years.
   - *Considerations:* Document menopausal status and add an LHRH agonist if she's pre- or perimenopausal. Get a baseline ECG and electrolytes, repeat the ECG around day 14, and check her antidepressants for QT prolongation or CYP3A interactions. Monitor liver tests, and watch for interstitial lung disease (she smokes). In advanced disease, an observational cohort found shorter progression on CDK4/6 inhibitors in BRCA or PALB2 carriers, and a prospective circulating tumor DNA analysis in a phase 3b trial linked TP53 alterations to early progression on ribociclib (see Emerging evidence). These don't change the adjuvant label. FGFR1 amplification, a possible resistance marker, wasn't tested.
   - *Why first:* An FDA label for exactly her setting, backed by a phase 3 trial with an invasive disease-free survival benefit (hazard ratio 0.749). She has no factor in the record that rules it out.
2. **Continue anastrozole alone (rank 2).** Arimidex (anastrozole) is labeled for adjuvant treatment of postmenopausal women with HR-positive early breast cancer (label 2026-03-02).
   - *Considerations:* Postmenopausal status needs to be documented (see Information gaps).
   - *Why second:* Standard therapy, but without the recurrence reduction that adding ribociclib showed for her risk group. It's the choice if ribociclib is declined, contraindicated (e.g. QT prolongation) or not tolerated.

The trial of giredestrant in place of the aromatase inhibitor (below) is an alternative to rank 2, not to rank 1. Co-occurring-variant evidence was checked for her alterations: none applies to the combinations she has.

**Not indicated by the patient's findings**

- **Adjuvant olaparib (Lynparza).** Labeled only for germline **BRCA**-mutated HER2-negative high-risk early breast cancer (label 2025-07-10). PALB2 isn't BRCA1 or BRCA2, and her germline panel found no BRCA variant. The pivotal trial's high-risk definition after adjuvant chemotherapy for HR-positive disease was 4 or more positive nodes; she had none. The genetic report's "olaparib, level B, breast cancer" rests on evidence in advanced disease. ASCO, ASTRO and SSO found data insufficient to recommend PARP inhibitors in the early setting or in moderate-penetrance carriers (2020). **Not recommended** in the adjuvant setting.
- **Adjuvant abemaciclib (Verzenio).** Its adjuvant indication requires node-positive disease (label 2026-09-18); she is node-negative.
- **Talazoparib (Talzenna).** Labeled for germline BRCA-mutated advanced or metastatic breast cancer only (label 2026-10-28).
- **NTRK inhibitors (larotrectinib, entrectinib, repotrectinib).** Tissue-agnostic labels require an NTRK gene **fusion**. Her NTRK3 change is a single-base splice-site change, not a fusion. CIViC evidence (level C) is that NTRK point mutations don't predict response. Fusions weren't tested.
- **Other tumor-agnostic options.** Her findings were matched against therapies and trials open to any solid tumor. No BRAF V600E was found. RET fusions weren't tested. HER2 is negative by IHC. MSI and TMB weren't measured. None applies.
- **Tamoxifen** isn't her current therapy (she takes an aromatase inhibitor), but her TP53 finding bears on it. CIViC level B evidence (accepted) links TP53 DNA-binding-domain mutations, a region that includes V173, to reduced tamoxifen response in advanced breast cancer: 2 of 11 patients with such mutations responded, against 66% with normal TP53. The supporting study counted only mutations at residues that contact DNA, which V173 is not. Weigh this if a switch to tamoxifen is ever considered.

**Later decision: if the disease recurs as advanced HER2-negative breast cancer**

1. **Talazoparib, off label.** CIViC level B evidence supports sensitivity in pretreated HER2-negative breast cancer with germline PALB2 variants: in a phase 2 study, 3 of 6 PALB2 carriers responded, all with breast cancer (EID 10856). A second CIViC level B item (EID 7460) is an earlier abstract of the same patients, so it isn't independent support. FDA labels for talazoparib and olaparib cover only germline BRCA carriers. Olaparib has no CIViC level A or B support in PALB2 breast cancer; its 2026 phase 2 data are under Emerging evidence.
2. **Trastuzumab deruxtecan (Enhertu)** is labeled for metastatic HR-positive breast cancer that is HER2-low (IHC 1+ or 2+ with negative ISH) or HER2-ultralow, after progression on endocrine therapy in the metastatic setting (label 2026-05-15). Her HER2 is recorded only as negative, without an IHC score, so whether she would qualify is unknown.
3. Pan-cancer CIViC level B items for TP53 mutation are set aside: VEGF/VEGFR inhibitors (EID 11709, 500 patients with mixed tumor types) and eprenetapopt with pembrolizumab (EID 12033, submitted; clinical benefit in 4 of 29). Neither is in breast cancer, and neither is a labeled option.
4. Trials of DNA-repair-targeted drugs for PALB2 carriers, and of ARID1A-targeted drugs, are open only for advanced disease (e.g. NCT04890613, NCT06488378, NCT04104776, NCT05490472).

## Emerging evidence

Searches covered 2025-07-10 to 2026-11-16 (PubMed; FDA announcements; ASCO, ESMO and AACR abstracts by web search). No new FDA approval for adjuvant HR-positive breast cancer or for PALB2-associated breast cancer was found.

- **Olaparib in metastatic breast cancer with germline PALB2 variants.** TBCRC 048 expansion cohorts, *J Clin Oncol* 2026 (phase 2, single arm). In 24 patients, response rate 75% and median progression-free survival 9.4 months. Strengthens the PARP-inhibitor option should she relapse; it doesn't address adjuvant use.
- **NATALEE 5-year results.** *ESMO Open* 2025 (phase 3 follow-up). Benefit of adjuvant ribociclib persisted (hazard ratio 0.716), including in node-negative patients (hazard ratio 0.606), with a numerical overall-survival trend (hazard ratio 0.800). Supports rank 1 for a node-negative patient like her.
- **BRCA/PALB2 carriers on CDK4/6 inhibitors.** *Breast* 2026 (French multicentre cohort, observational, metastatic first line). Of 4,283 patients, 80 carried BRCA1/2 or PALB2 variants, with shorter progression-free survival (adjusted hazard ratio 1.61). It doesn't remove the adjuvant benefit shown in NATALEE; no NATALEE results by PALB2 status were found. It's a reason to discuss, not to withhold.
- **TP53 in circulating tumor DNA and ribociclib.** BioItaLEE, *Clin Cancer Res* 2026 (phase 3b, advanced disease). Baseline TP53 alterations were linked to early progression on first-line ribociclib with letrozole. Prognostic, advanced setting only.
