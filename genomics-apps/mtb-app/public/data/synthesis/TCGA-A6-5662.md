## Cancer drivers

**Bottom line: a typical colorectal pattern drives the tumor: KRAS G12V switched on with the APC, TP53, SMAD3, RB1 and STAG2 brakes lost. None is targetable with an approved drug in colorectal cancer; KRAS G12V is the lead for trials.**

| Pathway | Gene | Role | Change | Treatment status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| RTK/RAS growth signaling | KRAS | Accelerator | G12V | Trial only (see [Treatment options](#treatment-options)) |
| WNT | APC | Brake | E1322* (truncating) | Watch |
| TP53 | TP53 | Brake | Y107D (hotspot) | Watch |
| TGF-β | SMAD3 | Brake | Two-copy deletion of its last exons | Watch |
| Cell cycle | RB1 | Brake | R556* (truncating) | Watch |
| (cohesin; not in the pathway set) | STAG2 | Brake | Frameshift | Watch |

KRAS G12V is a switched-on accelerator: it keeps the RAS growth signal on and, as in most colorectal cancers, works alongside loss of the APC brake in the WNT pathway, the usual first step in colon tumors. TP53 Y107D sits at a recurrent cancer position in the cell's main damage checkpoint. SMAD3, a brake in the TGF-β growth-restraining pathway, has lost both copies of its last exons, so the gene can no longer make a complete protein. RB1 R556* removes a cell-cycle brake, and STAG2 (a cohesin gene on the X chromosome, of which men carry one copy) is cut short by a frameshift. TP53 (93% of reads) and APC (88%) appear in a high fraction of reads, which is consistent with loss of the other copy, but no copy-number change was recorded at either gene and tumor purity isn't given, so whether both copies are lost can't be confirmed. RB1 is at a lower fraction (18%), which may mean it is present in only part of the tumor. The app's Pathways view shows these drivers on the pathway diagrams.

## Emerging evidence

Searches covered 2025-01-06 to 2026-11-17 (PubMed, FDA announcements and approval news, ClinicalTrials.gov). Searches can miss items, and several sources were read as abstracts only.

- **Daraxonrasib approved for pancreatic cancer.** FDA approval on 2026-08-26 for metastatic pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma after at least one prior therapy (Drugs, 2026, "Daraxonrasib: First Approval"; label dated 2026-08-27). Colorectal cancer is listed as in development. *Bearing:* not labeled for him; available through the RAS(ON) trials above.
- **Zanzalintinib plus atezolizumab (STELLAR-303).** Phase 3, previously treated metastatic colorectal cancer that is not MSI-H/dMMR: overall survival 10.9 vs 9.4 months with regorafenib (HR 0.80) (Lancet 2025). The FDA extended its review to a target date of 2027-03-03 (OncLive, 2026-09-11; Targeted Oncology, 2026-09-11). *Bearing:* a possible later-line option if approved; not available now.
- **Trifluridine/tipiracil plus bevacizumab every 14 days (PRABITAS).** Phase 3 in Japan: the 14-day schedule was non-inferior for overall survival to the standard 28-day schedule, with less grade ≥3 neutropenia (13% vs 39%) (Lancet 2026). *Bearing:* relevant to the later-line regimen.
- **Onvansertib with FOLFIRI and bevacizumab.** Single-arm phase II in KRAS-mutant metastatic colorectal cancer after oxaliplatin and fluorouracil: response rate 26.4% overall; in a post hoc analysis, 10.0% in patients previously given bevacizumab vs 76.9% without (Ahn, J Clin Oncol 2025; published online October 2024, just before the search window, and found because its print date falls within it). *Bearing:* he has had bevacizumab, the subgroup with low response; investigational.
- **TP53 missense type and bevacizumab.** In 62 patients with KRAS-mutated, liver-only metastatic colorectal cancer given first-line chemotherapy with bevacizumab, transcriptionally inactive TP53 missense mutations were associated with longer progression-free survival (Ruzzo, Oncologist 2025; published online October 2024, just before the search window, found by its print date). *Bearing:* retrospective; the transcriptional class of Y107D wasn't determined here.
